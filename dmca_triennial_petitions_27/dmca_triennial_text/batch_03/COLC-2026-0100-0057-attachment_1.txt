Item B.  Description of Proposed New Exemption
Provide a brief statement explaining the nature of the proposed new exemption. The information that would be most helpful to the Office 
includes the following, to the extent relevant: (1) the types of copyrighted works that need to be accessed; (2) the physical media or 
devices on which the works are stored or the services through which the works are accessed; (3) the purposes for which the works need to 
be accessed; (4) the types of users who want access; and (5) the barriers that currently exist or which are likely to exist in the near future 
preventing these users from obtaining access to the relevant copyrighted works.
Petitioners need not propose precise regulatory language or fully define the contours of an exemption class. Rather, a short, plain 
statement describing the nature of the activities the petitioners wish to engage in will be sufficient, as proponents will have the opportunity 
to further refine or expound upon their initial petitions during later phases of the rulemaking. A complete legal and evidentiary showing 
supporting this new exemption petition should accompany the comments in support of requested exemptions during the round 1 
comment period. Those seeking to submit comments in response to a petition depend on proponents fulfilling the appropriate evidentiary 
requirements, so providing a complete legal and evidentiary show in support of this new exemption is critical.

Types of works: Motion pictures and television programs whose only or primary authorized release is on physical optical disc
(DVD, Blu-ray, 4K UHD).
 
Media/devices: Optical discs protected by CSS, AACS, or AACS2, where playback requires dedicated disc-reading hardware.
 
Purpose: To permit format-shifting of a lawfully owned disc to a digital file for personal or controlled-circulation use, where the
hardware required to play that disc is experiencing continuing, accelerating decline in availability, consistent with the existing
standard applied to obsolete-format software and video games.
 
Users: Owners of lawfully acquired optical discs — including individual consumers and physical media rental libraries maintaining
circulating collections — seeking continued access to works they lawfully possess as compatible playback hardware becomes
progressively harder to obtain.
 
Barriers: Access to compatible optical disc playback hardware has been in continuous, accelerating decline rather than achieving
a fixed point of total unavailability. Manufacturers have progressively removed optical drives from computers, gaming consoles,
and televisions; multiple hardware manufacturers have discontinued production of dedicated disc players entirely, particularly for
4K UHD; and no manufacturer has introduced a new consumer optical disc format or expanded production in over a decade. While
used playback hardware for some formats remains findable on secondary markets, that supply is finite, non-replenishing, and
subject to ongoing depletion through failure and attrition, with no new manufacture to sustain it — particularly acute for 4K UHD
players, where multiple major manufacturers have ceased new production entirely. Section 1201(a)(1)(C) directs the Office to
consider not only present adverse effects but whether users are likely to be adversely affected during the succeeding three-year
period. Given this trajectory, the relevant question is not whether compatible hardware is available today in some diminished form,
but whether it will remain reasonably available through 2030, when the exemption sought here would expire absent renewal. The
continuing trend strongly indicates it will not. This circumstance is distinguishable from digital lending models rejected in prior
copyright litigation involving literary works, where a functioning licensed digital alternative existed in the marketplace and the
disputed activity was found to substitute for that existing, accessible market. Here, no such functioning alternative exists or is
emerging: as compatible playback hardware disappears, the underlying physical rental and sale market for these works is itself
becoming non-functional, independent of any action by the petitioner. The requested exemption would not displace an accessible
licensed market; it would preserve access to works that would otherwise become permanently unreachable through any channel,
licensed or otherwise, as the format's supporting hardware disappears.

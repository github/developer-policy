Trimedx is a third-party independent service organization with customers ranging from small rural hospitals to major metropolitan 
health systems. Our mission is to provide timely and cost-efficient clinical engineering and medical device repair and maintenance 
services that enable the continuity of patient care at our customer facilities.
To effectively provide service, Trimedx must be able to access and utilize both the medical devices themselves and any related 
service materials, including software and computer programs, service manuals, diagnostic software, patches, and service keys, 
that provide information necessary for the required maintenance and repair of those devices and enable the application of repair 
and maintenance procedures in accordance with original equipment manufacturer (OEM) performance specifications.
Unfortunately, OEMs often preclude and/or make such access difficult, forcing hospitals and third-party servicers to accept higher 
costs, longer service delays, and reduced autonomy over their own equipment.
OEM restrictions on repair access take many forms, including denial of access to service manuals, diagnostic and calibration 
software, proprietary software keys and licensing, restrictions on parts availability, specialized tools, and training. By their very 
nature, these restrictions are anti-competitive and designed to protect manufacturers’ revenue, not to advance patient safety or 
public interest. OEMs claim that such restrictions are necessary to protect their intellectual property, but in reality, such restrictions 
only serve to increase downtime and decrease device availability, ultimately leading to delays in patient care. 
Within the past year, Trimedx encountered a situation where an OEM prevented access to necessary software keys required to 
perform assembly, installation, adjustment, and testing (AIAT) procedures on a medical device, as mandated under 21 CFR 
1020.30. The device could not be properly serviced without these keys. The OEM's initial response was not to grant access, but to 
offer to provide the labor themselves, at a significant cost premium. This forced Trimedx and the hospital to choose between 
accepting OEM-only servicing at inflated costs or leaving the device inoperable. Only after extended negotiations between 
Trimedx and the OEM was a resolution reached and necessary access to provide the required regulatory compliance services by 
Trimedx was obtained.
This case exemplifies the core problem the DMCA exemption is designed to address: OEMs use access restrictions as leverage 
to control service markets and extract additional revenue at inflated costs, even when third-party servicers are capable, willing, 
and sometimes legally required to be able to perform the work.
The exemption to the DMCA allows Trimedx and other third-party servicers to overcome OEM access restrictions, ensuring that 
third-party service organizations have the necessary access to medical devices and associated information and tools required to 
ensure patient safety and regulatory compliance and to repair and maintain equipment on behalf of hospital customers.
Based on the foregoing, Trimedx respectfully requests that the Copyright Office continue and renew the exemption under 17 
U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1) that allows circumvention of technological measures applied to software programs and data files in medical 
devices for the purpose of diagnosis, maintenance, repair, and regulatory compliance of such devices. 
This exemption is not a grant of new privileges, rather it is a recognition that third-party servicers must have the ability to access 
the tools and information necessary to perform legally mandated and economically essential repair and maintenance work, 
without being blocked by access restrictions that serve no legitimate purpose other than to enhance manufacturer control and 
revenue.
Maintaining this exemption protects competitive markets, ensures hospitals have genuine choice in service providers, enables 
regulatory compliance, and keeps medical devices in service where they belong: patient care settings.
